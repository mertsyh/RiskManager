Step 2: Problem & Population

Problem Statement
Approximately 70% of COM0463 student project charters contain risk registers with fewer than three risks and no probability or impact ratings. Roughly 60% of student teams report during retrospectives that they failed to monitor scope creep during the semester, discovering deviations only at final review. Abstract lecture-based instruction in risk identification and scope management produces insufficient internalization of iterative project monitoring behaviors.
Target Population
Year 3–4 Computer Engineering and Management Information Systems students enrolled in COM0463 (IT Project Management)
Success Criteria
  • Achieve an average Victory Score of ≥ 4,000 points across the student cohort during the classroom simulation.
  • Ensure ≥80% of students successfully utilize the team Synergy mechanic to mitigate specific project risks during their playthrough.
  • Produce a ≥20% improvement in the Assignment 2 risk register completeness rubric score compared to the prior-semester control group.
  • Attain a ≥4.0 out of 5.0 mean satisfaction score on the L1 post-session survey evaluating enjoyment and PM concept clarity.
  • Achieve at least a 70% completion rate where students successfully finish a full 30-day simulation playthrough.
  • Increase replay behavior by ensuring ≥50% of students voluntarily attempt a second playthrough within the session.

MDA Analysis
  • mechanics: Players interact with the Risk Card system to select PMBOK-aligned responses (Mitigate, Avoid, Transfer, Accept), manage a $100,000 budget, hire from 8 distinct specialist roles, and advance daily project cycles where progress is calculated using productivity, morale multipliers, and synergy bonuses. The Upgrade Shop allows permanent risk-probability reductions, and the Event Log records all decisions for continuous monitoring.
  • dynamics: Aggressively accepting risks to save budget creates compounding failure states and cascading bug events later in the project, while heavy investment in risk mitigation and team morale generates high-velocity productivity loops — forcing players to constantly balance short-term budget conservation against long-term risk exposure. EMV-based comparison of concurrent risks drives prioritisation habits that mirror real PM practice.
  • aesthetics: The simulation primarily evokes Challenge through tight budget and deadline constraints with no safe dominant strategy, while intense visual feedback (screen shakes, warning vignettes, budget flash animations) creates Sensation — making the abstract consequences of poor risk management emotionally impactful and educationally memorable.

Step 6: Narrative & Concept Mapping

Narrative Theme
RiskManager: IT Project Management Simulation Game situates the player as a Project Manager responsible for delivering PROJECT: NEON — a real software product under simultaneous budget, deadline, and team-morale constraints. The game's design is governed by five evidence-based frameworks. Bloom Taxonomy structures the cognitive arc: early turns engage the Remember and Understand levels (naming the four PMBOK response strategies, reading the EMV formula on risk cards), mid-game escalates to Apply and Analyze (computing optimal responses under budget pressure, balancing the Iron Triangle), and the Post-Mortem report targets Evaluate (judging one's own decisions against measurable project outcomes). Self-Determination Theory shapes all motivational mechanics: Autonomy is embodied in the player's unrestricted choice of risk response strategy and team composition; Competence grows through the live EMV indicator and the personalised Post-Mortem feedback paragraph; Relatedness is built through the named employee roster, synergy bonuses, and the shared team-morale resource. Mechanics-Dynamics-Aesthetics disciplines the design layer: the Day Advance mechanic, four PMBOK response buttons, employee overtime, and upgrade purchases (Mechanics) produce emergent behaviours of risk prioritisation, budget triage, and Burnout avoidance (Dynamics) that yield aesthetics of Challenge and Discovery. HEXAD informs player-type coverage: Achiever types target the Victory milestone and budget surplus; Explorer types discover hidden synergy pairs and conditional event chains; Philanthropist types engage the eight-card educational sidebar; Disruptor types probe all three Game Over conditions deliberately. Kirkpatrick four-level evaluation governs outcome measurement across the deployment lifecycle: Level 1 Reaction is captured by a post-session satisfaction survey; Level 2 Learning by a short PMBOK quiz testing EMV recall and strategy mapping; Level 3 Behaviour by instructor observation of risk vocabulary use in subsequent coursework assignments; Level 4 Results by cohort-level project delivery rates in end-of-term capstone modules.
Game Overview
Risk Manager is a 30-day simulation game where players manage a software development project called PROJECT: NEON. Players must hire and manage a team, handle daily risks and events, make strategic decisions through dilemmas, and deliver the project on time while maintaining budget and morale.

Why (Motivation)
Students consistently underperform on risk management assignments because they engage with risk as an abstract checklist. The game creates emotional memory of risk consequences through visceral visual feedback — red vignette, screen shake, budget counter flash — which is more pedagogically durable than passive lecture. Research on game-based learning demonstrates significantly higher retention of procedural knowledge such as risk decision-making when compared to traditional instruction alone (Mayer 2019; Plass et al. 2020).
How (Ethics)
The simulation employs a strict compassion model: no personal data is stored, and failure states are purely instructional with losePoints=0 to maintain a zero-penalty learning environment. All gameplay data is anonymised by student number, and findings are used exclusively for educational research and simulation improvement. Students participate voluntarily and face no academic penalty for in-game decisions.
